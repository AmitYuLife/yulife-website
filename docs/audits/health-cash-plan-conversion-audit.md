# Conversion Audit — Health Cash Plan (Template Variant B, the fork)

**Audited:** 1 August 2026 · **Page type:** Product page (B2B) · **Primary goal:** "Get a quote" · **Primary audience:** Employer / HR buyer, SME-skewed (champion)
**Conversion Audit Score: 73/100**

> Scope: the desktop mid-fi wireframe of the dedicated Cash Plan fork. Mobile is not yet designed and page-speed is not buildable, so those two areas are scored provisionally and flagged, not failed.

> Verdict: this fork out-converts the insurance template on the thing that matters most, message-match. It leads with a concrete everyday benefit (cash back plus daily rewards), foregrounds the app-engagement story the champion buys on, and correctly drops the board/analytics bands. The two real gaps are proof credibility (its punchy stat bar and value figure float without sources, and the only testimonial is brand-to-brand, not a named customer) and the dense "What's covered" tri-column that is the biggest mobile-reflow risk.

## Top 5 issues (ranked by impact on conversion)

1. **The stat bar and headline value figure float without sources.** — "80% engagement · 1 in 2 daily · 99.5% of claims paid in 5 days · 12% reduction in absence", and the "80%" in "For your employees", appear with no attribution on the page. YuLife's own trust guidance is explicit: sourced stats are credible, floating stats are weak. · **Why it costs conversions:** unsourced numbers on a benefits page read as marketing rather than evidence, and a sceptical buyer discounts them. · **Fix:** add a source caption under the stat bar and the value stat (platform data / claims data, with year). · **Lens:** business goal + user need aligned; a compliance check on each figure is the only constraint.

2. **The only testimonial is brand-to-brand, not a customer.** — "A benefits package designed for humans by humans" attributed to "Bupa × YuLife" is YuLife quoting itself. · **Why it costs conversions:** peer proof from a named employer de-risks the purchase; a self-quote does not. · **Fix:** replace with a named-customer quote (role, company), or reframe the Bupa line honestly as a partner statement and add a separate customer testimonial. · **Lens:** business goal; depends on securing a real client quote (permission constraint).

3. **No review-score trust bar above the fold.** — The insurance pages carry Trustpilot / Capterra / App Store scores in the hero; this fork moved proof into the stat bar below it, leaving only the Bupa logo above the fold. · **Why it costs conversions:** the first viewport is where proof does the most work; the Bupa logo proves the underwriter, not satisfaction. · **Fix:** add the review-score strip into or directly beneath the hero, keeping the stat bar as reinforcement. · **Lens:** user need; no conflict.

4. **The "What's covered" tri-column is a dense read and a mobile risk.** — Three columns of seven-plus itemised benefits front-load a lot of text, and on mobile they must collapse to a single stack without becoming an endless scroll. · **Why it costs conversions:** cognitive load early in the page can stall momentum toward the quote, and a broken mobile reflow here would be the worst-hit section. · **Fix:** consider progressive disclosure (grouped, expandable) and design the mobile stack deliberately; keep the "Get a quote" CTA reachable throughout. · **Lens:** user need vs completeness (the plan genuinely covers a lot); resolve with disclosure, not deletion.

5. **Mobile is undesigned, and the page can't clear ~70 with confidence until it exists.** — Desktop-only wireframe; above-the-fold mobile CTA and tap targets unverified. · **Why it costs conversions:** SME buyers skew to mobile first touches; a buried mobile CTA silently caps conversion. · **Fix:** design the mobile fork next, tri-column first. · **Lens:** technical constraint; sequencing.

## Quick wins (fixable in under 1 hour)
- Add source captions under the stat bar and the "80%" value stat (platform / claims data + year).
- Add the Trustpilot / Capterra / App Store review strip to the hero.
- Reframe or replace the "Bupa × YuLife" testimonial so the page carries at least one named-customer voice.
- Strip **em dashes** from the drafted copy (YuLife tone of voice does not permit them) and confirm curly quotes and UK spelling throughout.
- Add a client-logo slot near the top so recognisable employers can be shown once available.

## A/B test recommendations
Ordered by expected impact × ease.

1. **Sourced vs unsourced stat bar.** Hypothesis: adding attributions raises trust and quote-CTA click-through. Metric: hero-to-"Get a quote" click rate; ideally paired with a trust-perception survey.
2. **Review strip in hero vs not.** Hypothesis: review scores above the fold lift the primary CTA rate. Metric: primary-CTA click-through.
3. **"What's covered" full tri-column vs progressive disclosure.** Hypothesis: grouped/expandable cover reduces early drop-off and speeds progress to the quote. Metric: scroll-through rate past the cover section and CTA reach.
4. **Named-customer testimonial vs brand line.** Hypothesis: a real employer quote converts better than the self-quote. Metric: CTA click after the testimonial band.
